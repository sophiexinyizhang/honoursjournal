Making Public Information Actually Accessible To The Public Is The Responsibility of Designers

AIGA Eye on Design

EVALUATION OF SOURCE

As a design-led editorial platform, Eye on Design is published by AIGA, the oldest and largest not-for-profit design organisation in the US. "We cover the issues important to the global design world and elevate the voices of contemporary designers as a way to build a more engaged design community." The examples the article expounds –the Mueller report and US electoral system–are US-centric, lacking a more geopolitically-diverse perspective. This article is published in 2021, already five years old but reflective of how important information accessibility in design is and even more so in today's contemporary political landscape.

SUMMARY

Berry and Martin use the Mueller report as an example of the "administrative aesthetic", dense unsearchable reports full of legalised jargon, footnotes and 12pt Times New Roman, to champion the power of good design to illuminate and inform. Here, they reimagine government documents as well-designed experiences, proposing integration of visualisation techniques, e-book indexing, digital storytelling or UX/UI web techniques. Thus, good communications design is championed as a means for translation of legal, inaccessible documents into public truth, aiding audiences in participating in our democracy and the accessibility of information.   "In the era of “fake news” and rampant disinformation campaigns, graphic design must address the gaps between information dissemination and the public’s ability to understand it."

INTERPRETATION

I've always believed good design is inherently user-centred, in that its primary purpose should be to communicate something effectively and authentically to its audience. The accessibility of information and education is even more critical in today's world where misinformation, fake news and polarising rhetoric dominate Trump's America–and where little is known in my generation's conscience of the broader political landscape, especially away from the US or Eurocentric world. I learned about Walter Benjamin's " politicisation of aesthetics" where art is functional and answers to politics. "Aesthetics should be responsible for and responsive to the true needs of the people."
